## 5 · How the seed was verified

Row counts are a weak check on data that travels as SQL text, so each table was compared against
the generated files by content, not by count:

- **`pb_facts`** carries a globally contiguous `created_at` sequence (6,469 values, one per row,
  `00:00:00.000` … `00:00:06.468`), which makes completeness exactly provable: no gaps, no
  duplicate timestamps. On top of that, an md5 over every column of every row, ordered by that
  sequence, matches the files — including notes and jsonb values.
- **`pb_accounts`, `pb_contacts`, `pb_signals`, `pb_deals`, `pb_calls`, `pb_identity_candidates`**
  were compared by an order-independent md5 over each row's columns (signals including their
  payloads), computed the same way on both sides.

That found six wrong rows out of 8,884, all from transcription in transit rather than from the
generator, and all repaired against the source files: a fabricated `pipedrive_lead_source` fact
for an account whose card carries no lead source; three facts whose `created_at` had drifted or
transposed; one fact whose note had been copied from its neighbour; one `notion_master` signal
stored with a Pipedrive-shaped payload, URL and enterer. Every table now matches its source
exactly. The comparison is cheap to repeat and worth repeating after any bulk load.
